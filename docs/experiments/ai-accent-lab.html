Is salmon right for AI features?

Salmon sits at hue 30; danger red at hue 25 — 5° apart. In a product where red means "Rejected", a warm-red AI/celebration accent risks reading as a warning. Toggle a dedicated AI accent for generative actions (Tailor · Coach · Generate) and watch the separation check below.

Read left→right: if the AI chip and the "Rejected" chip look like cousins, the accent is too warm/red. A cool AI accent (iris/teal) separates cleanly; the in-brand green ties AI to "Jobot working"; the warmed coral keeps warmth but pulls away from red.
The candidates
Green · in-brand
AI = "Jobot working." Max coherence, no new color. Trade-off: generative moments don't feel special.
Iris / violet
Industry convention for AI. Maximally distinct from green + red. Note: purple-adjacent — but a narrow functional accent, not a brand primary (≠ the rejected plum).
Teal · cool
Reads "smart/tech", calm, clearly not danger. Sits ~40° from green — keep it bluer so it doesn't muddy.
Coral · warmed salmon
Keeps the two-accent warmth but shifts hue 30→45 (20° off danger). Fixes the clash without a 3rd color.
My read: two separate fixes. (1) Regardless of the AI decision, separate salmon from danger — either warm salmon to coral (h45) or deepen danger to a crimson (h18, toggle top-right). (2) For AI, if you want generative actions to feel distinct, a cool accent (iris or teal) reads "AI" and can't be confused with rejection; if you'd rather stay coherent, green says "Jobot is doing this." I'd avoid keeping warm-salmon as the AI signal specifically.
